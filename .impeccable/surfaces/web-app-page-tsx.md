# Surface brief: the app (conversation to referral slip)

Visitor mode: Operate.
Audience and job: the patient themselves, on their own phone, often low-literacy, possibly frightened, outdoors with patchy data. They need to know where to go, how long it takes, and how sure we are.
Action and proof: call or navigate to a facility that can treat them. Proof is the facility's own quoted text with its source, the registry mark where the match is confident, and an honest statement of what could not be verified.
Constraints: English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam; the emergency path works with no network; no government emblem or scheme logos; text on glass keeps contrast; low-end Android.
Chosen direction: the referral slip (the dice's lead of seven structures).
Unresolved: product name; native-speaker review of every non-English string; clinician review of emergency wording.

## Direction contract

THESIS: One answer you can show at the door. The slip owns the screen and a chat thread never buries it; it refuses the scrolling chatbot transcript of identical cards.
OWN-WORLD: India's highway wayfinding boards. Atkinson Hyperlegible for Latin, system fonts for the Indic scripts. Liquid glass only on floating chrome (top strip, composer, mic, sheets); clinical content sits on solid white panels. Signature: the road bar, where free-flow minutes are solid and traffic delay is hatched yellow, with the likely range bracketed.
STORY: The person sees where to go, how long, and how sure we are; believes it because the facility's own words are quoted beside their source; then calls or navigates, with what to say and what to carry on the same slip.
